What is a Head of AI / Chief AI Officer? A complete guide to the role

Published 22 July 2026 · David Veselý

Head of AI (also known as Chief AI Officer, or CAIO) is a leadership role accountable for making artificial intelligence deliver a measurable business result in the company, rather than just costs and uncoordinated experiments. It unifies AI activity under a single owner, sets priorities by impact, establishes rules for data and security, and drives AI deployment into live operation. It combines strategy, technology and change management into one accountability.

What a Head of AI actually does

The role Head of AI is not about following trends or running workshops. It is operational accountability for how the company handles artificial intelligence — from strategy through to measured impact. In practice, a Head of AI:

  • Owns the AI agenda. Is the single clear owner with whom accountability for direction, priorities and AI decisions stops.
  • Sets priorities by return. Out of dozens of ideas, picks the ones with a defensible impact on revenue, cost or risk — and stops the weak ones.
  • Establishes governance. Defines the rules for data, security, human approval and regulatory compliance (in the EU, the EU AI Act in particular).
  • Drives deployment into operation. Makes sure pilots turn into genuinely used workflows with an owner and measured KPIs.
  • Reports to leadership and the board. Translates technical progress into the language of business: what is running, what it saves or earns, what comes next.

The day-to-day moves between three worlds: discussions with leadership about priorities and budget, work with teams and suppliers on specific deployments, and ongoing oversight of risks and results. The Head of AI is the bridge between the leadership that decides and the engineering that builds.

Head of AI vs CTO vs CDO vs AI Engineer

The Head of AI is often confused with existing roles. The difference is in the focus — who is accountable for what, and over what horizon:

The role Main focus What they primarily deal with Strategy vs execution
Head of AI / CAIO The business impact of AI across the whole company Priorities, governance, return and getting AI into operation Both — bridges leadership and engineering
CTO Technology and product development as a whole Architecture, engineering teams, the technology stack AI is only part of a wider technical agenda
CDO (Chief Data Officer) Data as a company asset Data governance, quality, availability and use of data Prepares the ground for AI, but does not run AI products
AI / ML Engineer Implementation of specific solutions Builds, integrates and deploys models and AI agents Execution, not company strategy or mandate

Put simply: the CTO is accountable for technology, the CDO for data, the AI Engineer builds the solutions — but none of them has the mandate to own AI as a company capability with accountability for impact. That is exactly the gap a Head of AI fills.

What competencies the role requires

The Head of AI sits at the intersection of three areas. If one is missing, the role does not work:

1. Strategy and business

The ability to translate what AI can do into priorities with a return, and to defend them in front of leadership and the board. Without a business perspective, AI turns into an expensive collection of experiments with no impact.

2. Technology

Enough technical depth to judge feasibility, suppliers, architecture and risk. A Head of AI does not have to code, but must be able to tell what is realistic, what is hype and what it will cost.

3. Change management and governance

Introducing rules, adoption across teams, risk management and regulatory compliance. Deploying AI is largely an organisational change — people have to know when to trust an AI output and when to step in.

The Head of AI in the Czech context

In the US and Western Europe the Chief AI Officer role has existed in large companies for several years. In the Czech Republic it is only now emerging — for several reasons:

  • A later wave of adoption. Czech companies deploy AI at scale later, so the need for a dedicated owner is only arriving now.
  • Company size. Most Czech companies are mid-sized and do not have full-time work for a Head of AI — which is why the fractional (part-time) model makes sense here.
  • Regulatory pressure. The EU AI Act and the AI literacy obligation force companies to govern AI, not just use it — and that requires an accountable role.
  • A shortage of profiles. There are few people who combine business, technology and governance. Demand for the role is growing faster than supply.

The result is that many Czech companies have AI activity, but nobody owns it as a company capability. At headofai.cz I fill that gap as a fractional Head of AI for companies in the Czech Republic.

Frequently asked questions

Is Head of AI the same thing as Chief AI Officer?

Yes — in practice they are synonyms for the same role: the person ultimately accountable for artificial intelligence in the company. The title Chief AI Officer (CAIO) is more common in large corporations and in the Anglo-American world, while Head of AI is also used in small and mid-sized companies. The substance is the same: own the AI agenda, set the priorities and answer for the impact.

Does a Head of AI have to be able to code?

They do not have to write production code themselves, but they do need technical judgement — an understanding of what is realistically achievable with today's AI, what it costs, what the risks are and how to assess solutions and suppliers. Without technical depth the role becomes an empty manager; without a business perspective, an engineer without a mandate. It is precisely the combination of the two that matters.

When should a company hire a Head of AI?

At the point where AI starts to affect revenue, costs, company data, customers or how employees make decisions — and nobody carries clear accountability for it. As long as AI is only a side experiment, a dedicated role is not necessary. But as soon as AI activity grows without an owner, rules and measurement, the company needs someone to take it over.

What is a fractional Head of AI?

A fractional Head of AI is the same role performed part-time. It suits companies of 50–500+ employees that need experienced AI leadership but do not yet have full-time work or the budget for a full-time senior role. The company gets both strategy and execution with a defined mandate and deliverables, without the cost and risk of a hire running into millions of CZK a year.

How much does a Head of AI cost?

A full-time senior Head of AI in the Czech Republic costs on the order of CZK 3–4 million a year including overheads, plus several months of recruiting and the risk of the wrong hire. A fractional Head of AI costs a fraction of that — you pay for a mandate or a monthly retainer according to scope, typically from the lower hundreds of thousands of CZK.
