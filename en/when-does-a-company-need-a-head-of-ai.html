When your company needs a Head of AI (and when it does not yet)

Published 22 July 2026 · David Veselý

A company needs a Head of AI at the point where AI starts to affect revenue, costs, company data or decision-making — and nobody carries clear accountability for it. If AI experiments are multiplying at your company without an owner, priorities and rules, it is time to fill the role. As long as AI is only a side experiment with no impact on the business, it can wait.

7 signals that missing AI leadership is holding the company back

A company usually needs the role Head of AI not because of one big decision, but because smaller symptoms keep piling up. If you recognise three or more of these, you are missing a clear owner of AI:

  • Pilot projects die. Plenty of demos and pilots, but rarely does anything reach live operation and start producing a result.
  • Every department plays with AI its own way. Uncoordinated tools, duplicated experiments, and nobody has the overall picture of what is running in the company.
  • Nobody owns the AI budget. Money goes on tools and licences, but nobody is accountable for their return or for whether they make sense at all.
  • Compliance risk is growing. The EU AI Act and the AI literacy obligation apply, but nobody manages compliance or documents how AI is used in the company.
  • Company data is leaking into external services. Employees paste sensitive data into public AI tools without rules and without oversight.
  • Leadership cannot decide what to do first. There are plenty of ideas, but no priorities with an estimated impact — so nothing gets done properly.
  • Competitors are starting to get ahead of you. AI is changing your market, your customer experience and your costs, and you are responding at random rather than strategically.

When you do not need a Head of AI yet

Getting the timing of the role wrong costs money and produces no results. Let us be honest — in these situations a dedicated Head of AI does not make sense yet:

  • You are a small company. At a few dozen people, the owner or one capable enthusiast can handle the AI agenda without a dedicated leadership role.
  • AI does not affect your business yet. If it is side experiments with no impact on revenue, costs or customers, the role is premature.
  • Basic digitalisation is missing. Without working systems, processes and at least some usable data, AI leadership is building from the roof down. Foundation first.
  • You are only looking for a one-off solution. When you need one specific problem solved, a supplier is enough — not a permanent role.

This honesty is deliberate: if I tell you that you do not need the role yet, I save you both money and disappointment. A Head of AI only makes sense once AI genuinely affects the company's results.

Three ways to fill the role

Once you do need a Head of AI, you have three options. They differ in cost, in the degree of accountability, and in whether anyone actually takes over your AI agenda:

What the first 90 days with a Head of AI look like

A good Head of AI does not start with an endless audit. The first 90 days have a clear structure that leads from chaos to managed performance — at headofai.cz following the CIPHER method:

  • Diagnostic (CIPHER Snapshot). A readiness assessment and a map of where AI arises in the company, what it costs and where the risk sits — with priorities by estimated impact in CZK.
  • Governance. Rules for data, permissions and human approval, and EU AI Act compliance — before anything goes into operation.
  • Deployment. The first priority AI workers go into live operation on top of your systems, with defined KPIs.
  • Board report. A summary of the impact achieved against target and a recommendation for the next period.

The goal of the first 90 days is a clear owner of the AI agenda, approved priorities and the first measurable results — not a presentation for the drawer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know it is time for a Head of AI?

The clearest signal is that AI activity in the company is growing but nobody owns it. Pilot projects never reach operation, every department uses its own tools, and nobody is accountable for the AI budget or for EU AI Act compliance. Once AI affects revenue, costs or company data and clear accountability is missing, it is time to fill the role.

Do we need our data in order first, before we hire a Head of AI?

No. Perfect data is not a precondition — one of the first things a Head of AI does is precisely to map the state of the data, processes and risks. You do need basic digitalisation, though: working systems, processes and at least some usable data. If the company is still introducing basic tools, start there, not with AI leadership.

Isn't a consultant or an agency enough for us?

A consultant or an agency delivers a specific project or recommendation, but does not take on accountability for the company's entire AI agenda. A Head of AI has the mandate to decide across departments, to hold the priorities, governance and budget, and to answer for the impact. When you need one problem solved, a supplier is enough. When you need someone to own and run AI, you need the role.

When do we not need a Head of AI yet?

If your company is small, AI does not yet affect the business and it is all still side experiments, a dedicated role is premature. The same applies if basic digitalisation or usable data is missing — it makes sense to build the foundation first. This honesty is deliberate: getting the timing of the role wrong costs money and delivers nothing.

What happens during the first 90 days with a Head of AI?

The first 90 days have a clear structure: a diagnostic and readiness assessment (CIPHER Snapshot) with priorities by impact in CZK, setting up governance and EU AI Act compliance, deploying the first priority AI workers into live operation, and a board report with measured impact. The goal is a clear owner, approved priorities and first results — not a long audit without execution.
