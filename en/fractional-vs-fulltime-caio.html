Fractional Head of AI vs full-time CAIO: what pays off for a company

Published 22 July 2026 · David Veselý

A fractional Head of AI is an experienced AI leader a company hires part-time instead of full-time. They take on the same accountability as a full-time Chief AI Officer — priorities, governance, AI deployment and reporting to leadership — but only to the extent the company genuinely needs. For mid-sized companies it is the fastest and least risky way to get AI leadership without a year-round commitment and a multi-million hire.

What a fractional Head of AI is

“Fractional” means a fraction of full-time. So a fractional Head of AI is a full leadership role — not consulting, not a one-off project — simply performed at a scale that matches the company's real need. The model works because most mid-sized companies need experienced AI leadership, but not for a full five days a week.

The scope of involvement follows the phase of the engagement, not a fixed table:

  • The kick-off is more intense — while the AI agenda is getting started, typically on the order of one to two days a week.
  • The ongoing mode is lighter — a few days a month is enough for running the portfolio, the priorities and the reporting.
  • Accountability is the same as in a full-time role — the mandate to decide, to hold governance and to answer for impact. Only the time differs, not the degree of ownership of the agenda.

The scope is agreed in advance and can change over time as the AI agenda in the company grows.

Fractional vs full-time: a comparison

Both options bring senior AI leadership. They differ in what they cost the company, how quickly they start and what risk they carry:

Criterion A fractional Head of AI Full-time CAIO
Cost A monthly retainer or mandate — a fraction of the full-time cost The full annual payroll cost, in the millions of CZK
Speed of starting Days to weeks Months of recruiting a rare profile
Commitment Flexible, one month's notice Permanent employment
Seniority A senior profile from day one, with experience from real deployments Depends on who you manage to hire
Risk Low — a bounded mandate with deliverables The wrong choice is expensive and hard to undo

Specific cost figures for both options are covered in the article on Head of AI pay. Put simply: the fractional model turns a large fixed annual cost into a flexible expense that follows actual need.

Who the fractional model is for

A fractional Head of AI makes the most sense for companies at a particular stage:

  • Companies of 50–500 employees. Big enough for AI to affect the business, but without a permanent full-time workload for a dedicated role.
  • Companies where AI already affects results. Priorities, governance and deployment are on the table — but there is no full-time capacity for it yet.
  • Companies that want a senior profile. In a full-time hire they would either be out of reach for an experienced AI leader, or would spend months searching.
  • Companies that want speed without risk. They need results within months, not a long recruitment with an uncertain ending.

If you are not sure whether it is time for the role at all, the article on when a company needs a Head of AI.

Who the fractional model is not for

Honestly — a fractional Head of AI is not a universal answer. It does not make sense in these situations:

  • Large companies with a permanent full-time workload. When the AI agenda is big enough to occupy a person full-time along with a team, go straight for an internal Head of AI.
  • Companies where AI does not affect the business yet. If it is side experiments, you do not need a leadership role — fractional or otherwise — yet.
  • Companies looking for a cheap implementer. When you need one specific system built, you are looking for a supplier, not AI leadership.
  • Companies without leadership backing. Without a mandate and a leadership willing to decide, the role has no leverage, whether it is full-time or part-time.

What a fractional engagement looks like in practice

In practice a fractional engagement typically has two phases, following the model used at headofai.cz:

  • The opening sprint. A bounded mandate with clear deliverables — diagnostic and priorities, governance, and the first AI workers deployed into operation. It kicks off the AI agenda and proves the model works in the company.
  • The follow-on retainer. Ongoing management of the AI agenda as a fractional Head of AI — the portfolio of initiatives, vendors, adoption and board reporting, at a lighter monthly scope.

The sprint does not commit the company to continuing — the retainer only runs if the opening phase proved its value. The specific scope, deliverables and pricing of both phases are set out on the homepage.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fractional Head of AI?

A fractional Head of AI is an experienced AI leader a company hires part-time instead of full-time. They take on the same accountability as a full-time Head of AI — priorities, governance, AI deployment and reporting to leadership — but only to the extent the company genuinely needs. It suits companies that need AI leadership but have neither the workload nor the budget for a full-time role.

How many days a month does a fractional Head of AI work?

The scope follows the company's needs and the phase of the engagement, not a fixed table. At the start, when the AI agenda is being kicked off, involvement tends to be more intense — typically on the order of one to two days a week. In the ongoing mode, a few days a month is enough to run the portfolio, the priorities and the reporting. The scope is agreed in advance and can be changed.

What is the difference between a fractional Head of AI and a consultant?

A consultant or an agency delivers a specific project or recommendation, but does not take on accountability for the whole AI agenda. A fractional Head of AI has the mandate to decide across the company, to hold the priorities, governance and budget, and to answer for the impact — just part-time. It is a leadership role, not a deliverable.

Does the fractional model pay off more than full-time?

For companies without a permanent full-time workload for this role — typically companies of 50 to 500 employees — usually yes. They get senior AI leadership without a year-round payroll cost, without several months of recruiting, and with a flexible commitment. A company with a permanent full-time need, by contrast, will move towards an internal Head of AI over time.

Does a fractional Head of AI ever move to a full-time role?

Yes, and that is the natural goal. When the AI agenda grows into a full-time role, the fractional Head of AI helps find an internal successor, onboard them and hand over a working system — the register, the rules, the agents and the metrics. The point is not to create permanent dependence, but to hand a managed agenda to an internal owner.
